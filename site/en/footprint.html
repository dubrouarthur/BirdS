Frugal by default

Compute follows the size of the model: a small model needs far less of it for every word. That is where the electricity and water savings come from.

Method and sources

  • Short request. 2,000 tokens read (instructions, tools and question included, with nothing deducted for the cache) and 500 tokens written, the answer length Epoch AI uses for a typical query.
  • Bird S 4B, electricity. Estimated from tokens: 0.006 Wh per 1,000 tokens read and 0.10 Wh per 1,000 tokens written, i.e. 2 × 0.006 + 0.5 × 0.10 = 0.062 Wh, rounded up to 0.07 Wh. The in-app meter applies the same method to every answer.
  • Per-token values. Drawn from published measurements of model servers (ML.ENERGY Benchmark, 2025), cross-checked against “How Hungry is AI?” (2025) and scaled to 4 billion parameters, then multiplied by 1.72: the ratio Google measured between the whole service (host machines, idle capacity, cooling) and compute alone. Comparable scope to the Gemini figure below.
  • Bird S 4B, water. 0.07 Wh × 0.15 L of cooling water consumed per kWh (the water usage effectiveness our host published for 2024) = 0.0105 mL, rounded up to 0.011 mL.
  • ChatGPT. Average query: about 0.34 Wh and 0.32 mL (Sam Altman, “The Gentle Singularity”, June 2025).
  • Gemini. Median text prompt: 0.24 Wh and 0.26 mL (Google, August 2025, arxiv.org/abs/2508.15734).
  • Ratios. 0.34 / 0.07 ≈ 4.9: over 4 times less electricity than an average ChatGPT query. 0.32 / 0.011 ≈ 29: about 30 times less water. Against Gemini, comparable scope (whole service): 0.24 / 0.07 ≈ 3.4 and 0.26 / 0.011 ≈ 23. OpenAI does not publish its method.
  • Size and compute. 4 billion parameters against about 400 billion for a large dense model: about 100 times smaller, and up to 100 times less compute per generated word, in theory.
